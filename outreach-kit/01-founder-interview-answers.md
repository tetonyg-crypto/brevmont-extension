**Q: Who are you and what business did you start?**

I'm Yancy Garcia, founder of Brevmont — an AI sales assistant for car dealership reps. Brevmont works as an AI co-pilot inside the dealership's CRM, instantly drafting personalized customer texts, emails, and follow-up notes so reps respond faster and close more deals. Before building it, I sold cars for a living — over $26.8 million worth.

---

**Q: What's your backstory and how did you come up with the idea?**

I didn't come from tech. I came from the showroom floor. I spent years selling cars and got good at it — $26.8 million in sales — and the whole time I kept noticing the same thing: deals don't usually die over price. They die over follow-up. A customer leaves, you mean to text them back, and then five new ups walk in and the lead goes cold. The best closers I knew weren't always the smoothest talkers — they were the most consistent followers-up.

I started building little systems for myself to fix that. Eventually I realized the thing I'd built for me could help every rep in every store. That became Brevmont: take the part everyone's bad at — fast, personalized, consistent follow-up — and make AI do the heavy lifting, right inside the CRM the dealership already uses.

---

**Q: How did you build the first version / take it from idea to product?**

I built it around the real workflow, not a fantasy one. Reps live inside their CRM all day, so Brevmont meets them there — it reads the customer and vehicle context already on the screen and drafts the text, email, or note in seconds. I obsessed over the unglamorous details: does it sound like a human, does it match the rep's tone, does it actually save time versus typing it yourself. I tested it against my own old playbook — the messages that actually closed deals — and kept tuning until the output was something a top rep would actually send.

---

**Q: What were the biggest challenges?**

Two. First, trust — salespeople have been sold a thousand "magic" tools that waste their time, so the bar to earn a rep's daily use is brutally high. It has to work the first time, every time. Second, building software as a non-traditional founder. I had to learn a lot fast. But coming from sales is also my edge: I'm not guessing what dealership reps need — I *was* one.

---

**Q: How are you marketing / growing it?**

Honestly, by living in the industry publicly. I create content every day as @cardogvlogs documenting the real car business — the deals, the grind, the reality of the floor. That builds trust with the exact people Brevmont is for. The product and the audience grow together.

---

**Q: What have you learned that you'd tell other founders?**

Build for the customer you used to be. I'm not designing for an imaginary user — I'm designing for the version of me grinding on the showroom floor. If you've actually done the job your product serves, use that. It's the one advantage nobody can copy.

---

**Q: What's next for Brevmont?**

Getting it into more dealerships and giving every rep an unfair advantage on follow-up — the thing that quietly decides who hits their number and who doesn't.
