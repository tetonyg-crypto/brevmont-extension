## Pre-written answers (adapt to the actual query)

### Q: "What's an underrated sales tip?"
The most underrated skill in sales isn't closing — it's follow-up. I sold $26.8M in cars, and I can tell you most deals don't die over price; they die because nobody followed up. The rep who texts back within the hour beats the slicker rep who waits three days, every time. Treat the customer who left as seriously as the one in front of you. It's unglamorous and it's where almost all the lost money is.
— *Yancy Garcia, founder of Brevmont*

### Q: "How is AI actually useful in sales (vs. hype)?"
The honest answer: AI is great at the part salespeople avoid, and bad at the part that makes them great. It shouldn't replace the relationship — but it's perfect for killing the blank page. Writing a personalized follow-up takes mental energy reps don't have at 7 p.m., so it doesn't get sent. AI can draft a solid, personalized starting point in seconds that the rep edits and sends. Use it to make consistency effortless, not to automate away the human part.
— *Yancy Garcia, founder of Brevmont*

### Q: "Biggest mistake founders make / lessons from building a startup?"
Building for an imaginary customer. I came to software from the showroom floor — I sold cars for years before I built Brevmont — so I'm designing for the exact job I used to do. If you've actually done the work your product serves, that empathy is an advantage no competitor can copy. The flip side: don't let coming from the industry stop you from learning to build. Your domain knowledge plus new skills is the whole moat.
— *Yancy Garcia, founder of Brevmont*

### Q: "How do you improve customer follow-up / retention?"
Make the next touch a default, not a decision. Every customer who leaves should have a follow-up already scheduled before they're out the door — don't rely on willpower later. Second, remove the friction of starting: reps who follow up consistently always have a template or draft they personalize, not a blank box. Third, measure the leak — count how many leads got *zero* second touch this week. What gets measured gets followed up.
— *Yancy Garcia, founder of Brevmont*

### Q: "What's it like building a vertical/industry-specific SaaS?"
The advantage is empathy; the challenge is trust. Selling software into a skeptical niche like car dealerships is brutal — reps have been burned by a hundred "magic" tools, so the bar to earn daily use is sky-high. It has to work the first time. But because I actually sold $26.8M in cars, I'm not guessing what reps need — I lived it. Vertical SaaS rewards founders who've done the job, not just studied the market.
— *Yancy Garcia, founder of Brevmont*

### Q: "Tips for content creators / building an audience?"
Consistency beats virality. My account @cardogvlogs isn't huge by follower count, but it's *present* — I show up every day documenting the real car business, and that builds trust with exactly the people I'm trying to reach. Pick a narrow lane, show the unfiltered reality of it, and post relentlessly. A small, consistent, on-topic audience is worth more than a big random one.
— *Yancy Garcia, founder of Brevmont*
