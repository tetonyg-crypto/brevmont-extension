**Title:** What Selling $26.8M in Cars Taught Me About Why Dealership Follow-Up Fails

**Author:** Yancy Garcia, Founder of Brevmont

I sold $26.8 million in cars before I ever wrote a line of software. And in all those years on the floor, I learned something that still surprises people outside the business: most deals don't die over price. They die over follow-up.

We don't like to admit that, because price is a better excuse. "They found it cheaper down the street." "The payment didn't work." Sometimes that's true. But far more often, the customer simply drifted — and we let them. We meant to text them back. We meant to send the numbers. Then five new ups walked in, the day got away from us, and a deal we'd already half-earned quietly went cold.

Here's the uncomfortable math. The average rep talks to far more customers than they ever follow up with. Every one of those un-followed-up leads is a deal you already paid for — in marketing spend, in floor time, in the emotional energy of the first conversation — and then abandoned a few feet from the finish line. Follow-up isn't the boring part of the job. It's the part where the money actually is.

So why is it the thing almost everyone is worst at? After years of watching great salespeople — and being one — I think it comes down to three things.

**1. Follow-up competes with selling, and selling always wins.** A live customer in front of you will always feel more urgent than a text to someone who left yesterday. So the follow-up gets pushed to "later," and later never comes. The best reps I knew weren't necessarily the smoothest talkers. They were the most disciplined followers-up. They treated the customer who left as seriously as the one standing in front of them.

**2. Blank-page friction is real.** Writing a good follow-up message takes thought. What do I say? How do I reference their trade, their kid's soccer schedule, the specific truck they liked, without sounding like a copy-paste robot? That tiny moment of friction — figuring out what to write — is enough to make a tired rep skip it entirely. Multiply that by 40 leads and the whole pipeline silently rots.

**3. Consistency is invisible until it's gone.** One missed follow-up never feels like a catastrophe. It's the *pattern* that kills you — and patterns are hard to see from inside a busy week. No single dropped lead shows up on a report. The slow leak does, three months later, when the numbers don't add up and nobody can point to why.

The good news: because follow-up is a *system* problem, it has system solutions. Here's what actually moved the needle for me and the reps I've coached.

**Make the next touch a default, not a decision.** Every customer who leaves should have a follow-up already scheduled before they're out of the lot. Not "I'll get to it" — an actual next step on the calendar. When follow-up is the default, you stop relying on willpower at 7 p.m.

**Kill the blank page.** The reps who follow up consistently almost always have a starting point — a template, a saved message, *something* they tweak instead of writing from scratch. The personalization matters enormously (generic blasts get ignored), but you personalize *faster* when you're editing a draft than when you're staring at an empty text box. Reduce the friction of starting and the follow-up actually happens.

**Speed beats polish.** A good message in five minutes beats a perfect message that never gets sent. The customer who hears back from you within the hour remembers you. The one who hears back in three days has already test-driven somewhere else.

**Measure the leak, not just the wins.** Most stores obsess over closed deals and ignore the leads that never got a second touch. Flip it. If you can see how many customers left without a follow-up this week, you can fix it. What gets measured gets followed up.

None of this is glamorous. There's no secret close, no magic word track. Follow-up is the quiet, repeatable discipline that separates the reps who hit their number from the ones who can't figure out why they didn't. It's also the most coachable skill in the entire dealership — which means it's the biggest opportunity sitting in plain sight in your store right now.

I spent years learning this the hard way, one cold lead at a time. If you take one thing from a guy who sold $26.8M and watched too many deals slip away over a text he never sent: the customer who left is not gone. They're just waiting to see if you'll follow up. Most won't. Be the one who does.
