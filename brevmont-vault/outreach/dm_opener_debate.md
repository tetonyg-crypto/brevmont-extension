# LinkedIn DM Opener — Internal Debate

**Date:** 2026-06-02
**Owner:** Yancy
**Problem:** First 5 DMs went out as a straight pitch ("a tool I built…"). No pattern interrupt. GMs file it under "vendor DM, ignore" before they read line two. The video is strong but nobody watches it because the text around it sounds cold.

**Goal:** Not to hide the pitch. Lead with something that makes a GM read the rest of the message *before* they realize it's a pitch.

---

## The message we're replacing

> "Hey [first name], couple of my stores in Utah and Idaho are using a tool I built. Their guys just say one sentence about the customer and it writes the follow-up for them. Made a quick video. Free to use. What do you think?"

The kill word is in line one: **"a tool I built."** The GM's brain tags it as a vendor in the first 5 words and swipes. Everything good in the message (the video, "free to use," the one-sentence hook) is downstream of a dead first line.

---

## Perspective A — The GM who gets 20 vendor DMs a week

**What makes me archive instantly:**
- Any first line with "I built," "our platform," "we help dealers," "quick question for you" (everyone uses this, it's burned).
- A compliment that's obviously copy-paste ("love what you're doing over there!").
- Anything that smells like it's going to end in "got 15 minutes Thursday?"

**What makes me stop scrolling:**
- Something that sounds like another GM or a rep talking, not a vendor. Lowercase, a little messy, an actual opinion.
- A real number or a real pain I felt this week. "leads going cold," "month was soft," "follow-up."
- A take I disagree with — I'll open it just to argue.

**What I'd actually reply to:**
- A message where replying costs me one word. "yeah" / "what happened" / "you're wrong." If you make me write a paragraph, I won't.
- The pitch shows up, but *after* I'm already nodding. If line one earns the open, I'll forgive line two for being a pitch.

**Verdict from the chair:** The opener has to read like it came from inside the business, not from a software company. Peer venting beats vendor pitching every time. Make the reply one word.

---

## Perspective B — Sales psychology / cold-DM reply rates

Ranking the pattern-interrupt families by what actually moves reply rate in B2B cold DMs:

1. **Shared pain (highest reliability).** You name a problem the prospect is living *right now* before you name yourself. Triggers "this person gets it." Lowest risk, very high open-to-reply. The catch: the pain has to be specific (follow-up, cold leads), not generic ("growing your business").

2. **Industry insight / hot observation (high, voice-dependent).** "Everybody's pitching marketing when it's a follow-up problem." Reads like a peer thinking out loud. Reply rate is high *if* the voice is credible — falls flat if it sounds like a thought-leader LinkedIn post.

3. **Question opener (high, if it's a real question).** A genuine either/or question ("traffic or follow-up?") invites a one-word answer, which is the lowest-friction reply that exists. Dies if the question is rhetorical or obviously a setup.

4. **Controversy / hot take (high ceiling, higher variance).** "Most CRMs are expensive note-takers." Gets opens and arguments. Risk: you can offend a guy who just bought that CRM. Great for reply *volume*, noisier on reply *quality*.

5. **Compliment (lowest, most burned).** Works only if specific and earned ("watched how your store moves units"). Generic compliments are the most-archived opener in cold outreach. Include it as a variant for measurement, but expect it to lose.

**Data instinct:** shared pain and the real question will likely top reply rate; controversy will top reply *volume* with messier quality; compliment is the control/loser. We don't have to guess — that's what the rotation test is for.

---

## Perspective C — Yancy's actual voice

Yancy is 23, sold cars on the floor, not a polished SaaS founder. His edge is that he *was* the guy drowning in follow-ups. The message has to sound like a car dog texting another car dog.

What that means concretely:
- Lowercase-ish, contractions, "wth," "your guys," "the floor," "moves units."
- No "solution," "platform," "leverage," "streamline," "reach out."
- He can say "built a thing" / "built something" — that's a guy who made a tool in his garage, not a company shipping a product. Keep it small.
- He earns the right to have an opinion about follow-up because he lived it. Lean on that, not on credentials.
- The CTA is how a car guy ends a text: "worth a look?", "thoughts?", "tell me I'm wrong?" — never "open to a quick call?"

The opener Yancy reached for himself — *"wth happened to sales this month. Everybody is pitching marketing when it's really a follow-up problem"* — is exactly right for his voice. It's an industry-insight opener delivered as peer venting. It only works *because* it's him saying it.

---

## Where the three perspectives agree

- **Kill "a tool I built" from line one.** It's the single biggest fix.
- **Lead with the prospect's world** (their month, their leads, their floor), not ours.
- **Make the reply one word.** Friction kills B2B DM reply rate more than anything.
- **Keep the pitch — just demote it to line two**, after the open is earned.
- **Voice is the moat.** The same words from a "SaaS founder" account would get archived. From an ex-car-guy, they land.

## Where they disagree

- Psychology says **shared pain** is the safest bet. Yancy's gut says **industry insight**. The GM chair says **anything that sounds like a peer**, which both satisfy.
- Controversy has the highest ceiling but the most downside risk (offending a recent CRM buyer). Worth testing, not worth launching with.
- Compliment is the consensus underdog — keep it only as the control.

## Conclusion → the test

Don't pick one on instinct. Ship 5 variants, one per pattern-interrupt family, rotate them evenly, and let reply rate decide. Yancy's instinct (industry insight) is the launch default because it's the most authentically *him* and reads as peer-venting — but it holds the #1 slot only until the data says otherwise.
